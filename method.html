Where £241.30 comes from, and where our number diverges from yours

The forecast calculator on this site does one sum, the one DWP uses for National Insurance years after 5 April 2016. This page shows the sum, works it through for four records you can check yourself, and says plainly where it stops: if your record has years before April 2016, your gov.uk forecast is the figure to use.

Worked examples at 2026/27 rates

Each line is (qualifying years ÷ 35) × £241.30, the full rate from the DWP rate sheet1, with nothing paid below 10 years. These are our arithmetic, not anyone's forecast.

The single rule that drives every number on this site

Weekly amount = (qualifying years ÷ 35) × £241.30, capped at 35 years, and nothing under 10 years. Each year is worth £241.30 ÷ 35, about £6.89 a week. That is the rule DWP gives for anyone with no National Insurance record before 6 April 2016, and the amount each later year adds to a starting amount2.

Most people near State Pension age do have years before April 2016. For them DWP works out a starting amount: the higher of what they would have got under the old rules and under the new rules, with a deduction inside the new-rules figure for any years they were contracted out. If the starting amount is above the full rate, the extra is a protected payment3. The deduction depends on your earnings and scheme history, which only DWP holds, so this site does not estimate it.

Where this site is deliberately weaker than gov.uk

Two places. First, any record with years before April 2016: the starting amount and any contracted-out deduction come from DWP's own records, so use the weekly figure on your gov.uk forecast4 and, if it is above the full rate, the SERPS check on the homepage to split out the protected payment5. Second, State Pension age: the rise from 66 to 67 for people born 6 April 1960 to 5 March 1961 means a month of birth changes the date, so check the exact day with gov.uk's State Pension age service6. If you already draw your State Pension, your DWP award letter is more current than anything we can model.

When this changes

The numbers on this site move on three specific events: the Autumn Budget (any rate-sheet preview), the April uprating (triple lock applied to the headline figures), and any DWP-published SPA review outcome. We do not republish on news cycles or media speculation about the triple lock; we wait for the rate sheet. The next scheduled refresh follows the Autumn Budget 2026. Changes are listed on the updates page.

What we don't do

No financial advice. No affiliate links to pension providers, IFAs, or buy-back paid-help services. No data collection - the calculator runs in your browser and nothing leaves the page. Treat this site as the workings; treat gov.uk as the answer.
